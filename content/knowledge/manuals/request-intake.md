## What a request is

A request is one unit of work with an owner, a team and a status. Every approval, incident, purchase and customer escalation is a request, so there is one place to look for what is in flight.

## Choosing the team

- **Operations** for incidents, equipment, offices and access.
- **Finance** for money, invoices, purchases and pay.
- **Support** for anything a customer raised.

If two teams are plausible, file with the one that owns the outcome, not the one that owns the tool.

## What to put in the title

Write what you want to be true when the request closes. "Restore SSO for the Oslo team" is a title. "SSO broken" is a status update.

## What to put in the details

1. What happened, in one sentence.
2. Who it affects, and how many people or customers.
3. What you have already tried.
4. The deadline, if there is one, and why it exists.

Details are read by someone who was not in the conversation, so include the links and the reference numbers.

## Priority

Choose low, medium or high. High means a core workflow is blocked for a team or a customer commitment is at risk. Do not choose high to move faster in the queue; the support lead reviews priority on every high request.
